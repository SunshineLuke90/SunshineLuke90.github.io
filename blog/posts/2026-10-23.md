# Japan Trip - Day 13

Back on the road today. Whether I feel good or not, I have to make it back to Fukuoka, to drop the bike off and complete my rental. I got up early, around 7:30 (Hey that's early for vacation) and got on the bike to head out. I decided to stop at a Lawson in Beppu before I really got started, to fuel up, since I didn't plan on stopping anywhere else until Fukuoka. 

I got a Egg & Ham sandwich, vitamin C shot, and a bottle of Monster (I'm so glad they have bottled energy drinks here, it makes my day every time) to fuel up. Downed the vitamin C shot right away, and scarfed down the Egg & Ham sandwich. The conbini sandwiches here are so good, even if they are really simple. I'm not looking for anything fancy when I get one of these sandwiches, just looking for something tasty, and that will give me some fuel. These always do the trick. 

The route today was about 160km, which was on the longer end of the routes that I've taken. I didn't stop for anything really, but I did run into a really cool gorge, and a really cool scenic view with really tall pointy rocks on a cliff side. Hard to describe the second thing I saw, but I think I got it in my DJI action cam footage.

I did stop at one more Lawson on the way, I had to use the bathroom, and I also wanted to get some masks for the train ride, as it's best in Japan if you're feeling unwell and are going to be around people to wear a mask. Honestly, it's probably best practice anywhere in the world, but it's deeply ingrained in culture here, so I found some snoopy masks!

## Bike drop off

I pulled into the Rental 819 place in Fukuoka, and parked the bike. Luckily, the people working the shop were actually the same people who I picked up the bike from, so they recognized me. I pulled all of my stuff off the bike, and was in the middle of taking all of my gear off, when they checked the gas tank to discover it half full. I was supposed to refill it before dropping it off! I apologized, and asked if I could take it to a gas station right now to fix it. Thankfully they let me store my stuff in the store, and I took the bike to go to the gas station. 

Getting to the gas station was easy, but getting back from the gas station to the drop off was quite difficult. There was a massive 6 lane road that I would have to cross to get back, so I had to do a bit of a loop to take it back. No problem, I made it happen. I went to get my stuff, and one of the guys handed me my bags, although it seemed he was reluctant, or unsure about it. I figured he just wasn't sure they were my bags, so I took them with enthusiasm, but shortly afterwards, he did a bit of google translate and said there was a shuttle leaving soon, and he asked if I could change quickly. I think this is what he was implying when he handed me my bags.

I did need to change, especially since my jeans were quite wet from the ride, so I grabbed my replacement pants out of my bag, and quickly changed, then stuffed my boots and jeans back in my suitcase with vigor, trying to make it to the shuttle on time. Turns out, the shuttle was just for me, and he apologized if he was rushing me. Probably something lost in translation with the first request to change. I sorted the rest of my bag on the shuttle, as he took me back to the airport terminal. I needed to go to Hakata (train station), but I figured it was easier to just take the subway from the airport to Hakata anyways, since I did that on the way coming in. 

## Shinkansen Travel

At this point, I had taken ~6 trains, so I figured "I'm a pro" and turns out, I was! I figured out all of the train tickets first try, and I already had plenty of cash & coins ready to go for the fares. I easily made it on the first available subway to Hakata, and then bought my Shinkansen ticket at Hakata. On the way to Fukuoka, I got hungry about halfway through the trip. I had heard online that there are commonly Shinkansen bento boxes sold at the train station, so I found a place that sold bento boxes at Hakata, and bought one for the Shinkansen, then made it on the first Shinkansen leaving for Tokyo. So efficient!

The bento was quite good, though not super noteworthy. Only noteworthy thing is that the whole box was made of wood (or bamboo, couldn't tell), and wrapped up in paper with a rubber band holding it all together. That was really quite cool. The bento itself was nothing to write home about though. Just rice, tuna, egg and seaweed. As soon as I got on the train, I quickly ate the bento box, then put on one of my snoopy masks. Probably not best practice, as I didn't have the mask on the whole time, but much better than nothing.

## Getting to the Hotel

__I AM NOT A PRO__

I got off the Shinkansen, and then looked on google maps to figure out what train I needed to take to get to the hotel. Turns out, I got off at the wrong Shinkansen station (I got off a stop too soon), so I was further away than I expected. I wasn't sure if I could just wait for the next Shinkansen to come so that I could take it to Tokyo station, so I just went upstairs, and went out of the Shinkansen area, and my ticket was returned to me as I left. This was odd, because when I got off the Shinkansen in Fukuoka, the ticket was eaten when I left the Shinkansen area. I walked around for a while, trying to find a ticket booth that would let me buy a short train to Ueno, but I couldn't find one, and didn't want to go out of the main ticket gates. I eventually asked a JR assistant, and he basically said "Just get on the train, you're all good".

For context (take it with a grain of salt), when I had bought both Shinkansen tickets, they required both a Shinkansen fare, as well as an "express" fare. I got 2 separate tickets when going Tokyo -> Fukuoka, and only one ticket when going from Fukuoka -> Tokyo, but both were a 2 step process. Here's my guess: The "Express" fare is to allow you to take additional trains within the Tokyo metropolitan area, so you basically pay a single fare to be able to complete a full trip from point A to point B, across potentially multiple trains. I got on the subway line to Ueno, and got off at Ueno no problem. When I put my Shinkansen ticket into the exit gate, it let me out with a green check.

This was way more stressful and confusing than the previous encounter though, so I definitely wasn't sure what was going on. Put me in my place a bit, after having so few issues in Fukuoka.

## Hotel & Light Exploration

I checked into the hotel, super easy. The lady at the registration desk spoke perfect native English, which was a very different experience to what I'd had through my time in Kyushu, where people would be able to speak a little bit of English, but nothing fluent. I got into my hotel room, and it's by far the smallest actual room I've stayed in. I stayed in capsules the first 2 nights, but besides those, this place is TINY.

![My hotel room. Not much more than a bed and a desk](/blog/images/tokyoHotelRoom.JPG)

I was getting a bit hungry, so I decided to go for some ramen. I found a place nearby the hotel that was a ticket based place. I have tended to like the ticket based ramen places, since they're cheaper, and they also require less uncomfortable waiter interaction. I arrived, and there were at least 2 tourist couples already in the restaurant. To me, this isn't a great sign. I'm looking for the places that are frequented by Tokyo natives, not the touristy destinations. There were plenty of Japanese people here as well, but as time went on, the ratio moved to ~50-50 tourists to natives. This was super weird to me, especially since I had been in Kyushu for so long, where seeing a tourist was a rare sight. 

When I went up to the ticket machine, I was looking for a card option for payment, but it was a cash only machine, which was odd since there was a clear "Tap to pay" piece of paper taped to the top of the machine. The chef called out to me and told me to wait a second if I wanted to use a card, so I just sat at the bar. It only took a second for the guy to come over, and he just took my order verbally. I ordered a ramen set with a beer. Been ordering beer quite often, it's so cheap and I like Japanese beer. Sue me.

![Tokyo Ramen, with an Asahi beer in a glass mug](/blog/images/tokyoRamen1.JPG)

It was quite good! When reading the reviews on google, this place was highlighted for making their own ramen noodles in house, which was super cool, and I could definitely tell that they were homemade. They weren't as soft as most of the noodles I've had so far, more firm, but a good taste. The broth was super thick, and fatty, which was really good.

After having the Ramen, it was already around 8pm, so I just laid in the hotel room for the rest of the night. I still have a cold, so more rest is good.
