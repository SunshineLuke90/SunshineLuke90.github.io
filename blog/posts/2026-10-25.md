# Japan Trip - Day 15

It's a new day. I got up a touch earlier than yesterday, I'm starting to begin to feel better, but I don't feel the need to rush. My main goal for the day was to visit the staircase featured at the end of Your Name, where Mitsuha and Taki meet at the end of the movie. 

## Your Name Pilgrimage

My hotel is in Ueno, and the staircase, and many of the other sites featured in the movie are near Shinjuku. I left the hotel, and initially wanted to go back to the cafe that I had visited the day prior, but when I walked by, there was a line of tourists out the door. Oh well. 

I continued to Ueno station, and since I know I'm going to be in Tokyo for the rest of the week, and will be doing plenty of train travel, I decided to get a Suica card. Japan has a "Welcome Suica" which is basically just a pre-paid card to make it easier to get from point A to point B. What I'd been doing so far on the trip was going to a ticket station and buying a one way ticket, prepaid to just get me from point A to point B. The way you use these tickets, you get a physical ticket, which on one side has a magnetic strip, similar to how a credit card strip looks, but it's a flexible ticket. You then go to one of the entry gates, stick the ticket in, and it pops out the other end with a hole punch in it, so you walk through and grab it on your way through. You then take your trains, and when you get to your exit station, you put the ticket into the gate, and the gate eats it and gives you a check mark (hopefully).

The hard part with the ticket system, you have to punch in the exact station that you want to pop out at, and if you're doing a multi-leg journey, it can be a bit difficult to figure out what you need to put in. Plus, it means every time you need to use the subway, not only do you have to figure out your route on google maps, but you also have to manually plug it into a ticket booth every time. It was cool to do, especially for when I only needed to take a couple of trains on the way to Fukuoka, but now that I'm using it all the time, I wanted a Suica.

So why the Suica card? Basically, instead of having to figure out exactly how much ticket payment you need at the ticket booth, before you get on the trains, you just walk through the gate, and swipe your Suica card. What happens, is the Suica system notes which gate you entered and likely creates a "From" record tied to you, with no "Destination" record yet. Then, you just take whatever trains you want, to wherever you want, and swipe the Suica card as you leave. As soon as you do that, the system now has a "Destination" and can just look up how much that route costs using it's own lookup table, doing so effectively immediately. Then it just charges your account, and as long as your card has the funds, it lets you through. What an efficient system! Added bonus for me, I get to keep the card as a souvenir. It's got a nice red design, with flowers on it. 

Alright. So I wanted the Suica card. Luckily, Ueno station offers the card for tourists, as it's not sold everywhere. I was able to find the line to get one, and paid ¥5000 cash to pre-load it. Crazy thing, a Suica card doesn't just work for train fees. It also works for vending machines, and many convenience store purchases. This is a huge benefit, since one of the drawbacks to the tourist Suica card is that it's only valid for 28 days, and if you don't use all of the funds, you lose them. 

But, I got my Suica card nicely figured out, and had my destination plugged in. Yotsuya station. I didn't get a picture of the station itself, but the station is featured a few different times within the Your Name film, so it was cool to see, and travel the path of the characters from the film. I then began walking to the stairs where Mitsuha and Taki met. They're tucked away in the middle of a neighborhood, so I figured there likely wouldn't be too many people around and I could get some good photos. WRONG. I think there were probably 30 tourists surrounding the stairs trying to get pictures in varying capacities. I thought I was doing a hyper-niche activity by going to explore some random places from an anime movie from 2016, but I guess not.

The next destination was The National Art Center, in Tokyo. This destination was primarily part of the Your Name pilgrimage, but it was also an art museum, which I always find interesting. There were 3 exhibits in the museum, including one focusing on Renaissance pieces loaned from the Louvre. It was a really big exhibition, spanning 3 floors, and all of the pieces were huge, all at least 5 feet tall. 

Before I did that, I went all the way to the top floor, to collect my pilgrimage picture. When I got to the third floor, the spot where the picture should be taken from was being taken by someone already taking a photo. They were trying really hard to get a photo, so they took a while, and there was a couple waiting to take the photo from the right spot, so I waited in line basically. The first person took a super reasonable amount of time, like 30-45 seconds, but the couple that showed up next, they took a solid 5-10 minutes, taking photos with both of their cell phones, then with a film camera, then with some post cards, then of both of them as a selfie, then with one taking a picture of the other, then flip the other way, and then taking another 2-3 minutes just inspecting the pictures they had just taken, in the same spot where they were taking the photos. Can you tell my frustration? Once they moved, there were another 2 or 3 people besides me waiting to take a photo, but I was next, so I quickly got into the right spot, snapped my photo, and got out of the way. Less than 20 seconds. The way it's meant to be.

## Meji Jingu Shrine

I started going back to the subway, and took a couple of lines to make it to Meji Jingu shrine. When I got to Harajuku station, and started to head up for the shrine, I saw there was a Harry Potter store right above one of the exits. Why not. So I headed to the Harry Potter store, and got an incredibly overpriced butterbeer. It was very good. 

![The escalator going to the second story of the Harry Potter store in Harajuku](/blog/images/harajukuHarryPotter.JPG)

When I left the store and started heading to the shrine, there was clearly something going on at a nearby convention center. Probably an Anime convention? But there were thousands of people there, and more continuing to pour in. I crossed a bridge to get to the shrine area, clearly going against the flow of traffic, but that's just because there were so many people going to the convention. I decided not to head straight to the shrine, and instead to walk around the neighboring Yoyogi park. There were way fewer people than I expected there, even with it raining. It was a really big park, for being so close to Shinjuku and Shibuya. While I was walking around the park, it was hard to tell you were in such a big and dense city.

After walking around the park, for about an hour, I headed to the neighboring Meji Jingu shrine, which itself was about the same size as Yoyogi park. The difference being, Meji Jingu is a sacred place, and is more formal so going into the forests or trees is forbidden, and no running or bikes were to be found. 

![The path to Meji Jingu shrine](/blog/images/mejiJinguPath.JPG)

The Torii gates on the way into the shrine were huge, and were completely made of wood (just about). Some of the biggest wooden Torii gates that I've seen on the whole trip.

![A huge wooden Torii gate, leading to Meji Jingu shrine](/blog/images/mejiJinguTorii.JPG)

The shrine itself wasn't as big as I thought it would be, although it was very large. There was also a museum, and other supporting buildings, but I decided to just live in the moment and walk through and explore. The forests on all sides were so dense and pretty, and the trees were all so old. That's been one of the consistent themes in all of the shrines that I've been to, is that the greenery is old and dense. Really cool vibe every time.

## Tokyo Tower

After Meji Jingu, I realized I was somewhat nearby Tokyo Tower, so I took the subway tho go see it. I know you can go up in the tower, but that costs money, and doesn't sound all that interesting. Getting to see the tower itself though, was really cool. To me, the Tokyo tower is one of the most iconic pieces of architecture in Tokyo, and when I think of Tokyo, the building I think of is the tower. 

![A picture from the ground of Tokyo tower](/blog/images/tokyoTower.JPG)

I went into the gift shop/tourist trap/cafe at the base of the tower, and one of the shops had stickers, one for every prefecture. Score! I had been trying to collect a bunch of stickers from along my journey, but had been having a hard time in some of the prefectures I visited on the bike. This store had all of them, so I went ahead and bought one for each prefecture I had visited while on the bike. 

I also got a bowl of ramen here, which was pretty good, but a bit pricier than usual, likely due to the tourist trap nature of the place. I decided not to stick around too long, to prevent from getting tourist trapped too hard. 

At this point, it was already starting to get dark, so I took the train back to Ueno, to call it a night. On the way though, I came across a Famima merch shop. I have been loving Family Mart in Japan, probably my tourist's favorite place. I had heard before coming here that there would be themed stickers and socks for each prefecture, and I've checked every Family Mart I've been to for both, but haven't found a single one of either. This place still didn't have the themed socks, but it did have a Tokyo themed sticker, so I went for it.
