# Japan Trip - Day 4

![The white Triumph Speed 400](/blog/images/TriumphSpeed400.JPG)

Today is the day I pick up the motorcycle! I rented a Triumph Speed 400 from [Rental 819](https://rental819.com), and I had been so excited to pick up the motorcycle, and not have to carry all of my stuff with me everywhere I go. 

At this point, I fully understood how to use the ticket stations, and I had some coins to use at the machines, so I blitzed through the subway (地下鉄) station in no time. I did this so efficiently in fact, that I forgot that I needed to kill an hour before my pickup time was scheduled. Oops!

I decided to just show up an hour early and reap the consequences, if there would be any. Good decision too, because 2 others who were renting bikes showed up at almost the exact same time. I sorted the documentation, and we were shuttled to where the bikes were actually located, which was on the other side of the airport. An interesting decision to split the place where you show up to rent the bike, and where the bikes are actually kept. I would've thought you'd want them in the same place.

The guy who helped show me the bike, and the regulations was really nice. He kept apologizing for his bad english, but I had to reinforce that he had pretty solid english, and I could understand the gist of what he was saying the whole time, english or japanese.

I had decided to add on a top case to the motorcycle, so that I could put my bags in it and worry less about how to carry all of my stuff around. What a good decision! The top case was so big that it fit my entire duffel, and my whole backpack! 

## Starting to ride

I set off from the rental place, navigating to Sasebo using the Kurviger app. 

> Quick shout-out to [Kurviger](https://kurviger.com). It's a really good app for finding twisty routes to get from point A to point B. You can set the curviness of a route, so all you have to do is set point A and point B, and request a curvy route and it just works!

I took about 15 minutes to get used to the bike. The handlebars are a bit higher and closer than my Ninja, and the tank is much skinnier than my Ninja as well. Thankfully, the bike was very similar to the Harley-Davidson that I learned on during my MSF course a few years ago, so I wasn't completely lost. This bike has a lot of low end torque though, so you don't need much throttle to get going from a stop. I stalled a couple of times in the first 10 minutes of riding, but thankfully I didn't get beeped at or anything for it. Riding on the left was super easy too, just had to follow traffic, and traffic also goes quite slow, since the average speed limit is like 40kph. 

## Pre-mountains 711 Stop

As I was about to leave Fukuoka, I stopped at a 711 to get some food in my system. I got a pretty simple meal, with 2 tuna mayo onigiri, a red bean paste bun, an aluminum bottle of Red Bull, and a water. What a meal! The red bean paste bun was very good, and the onigiri were just what I had come to expect. I had never seen Red Bull in an aluminum bottle, but it tastes just like red bull so I'm more than happy!

![My breakfast meal. Tuna mayo onigiri, red bean paste bun, Red Bull and a water, on a countertop](/blog/images/711Breakfast.JPG)

## Into the mountains

My route for the day took me through the mountains. Quickly I realized I was on a popular route, because there were many motorcycles passing by in both directions. The turns were incredibly tight too, many hairpin turns, and lots of steep inclines. I had no issue riding any of it, although it was definitely the most technical riding that I've ever done. 

![Scenic overlook across a Japanese forest](/blog/images/forestOverlook.JPG)

Early on into the ride, after stopping once or twice for scenic overlooks, I ran into a motorcycle accident. It was an older guy (guessing 70s or 80s) who had crashed on a maxi scooter, and was laying amongst a bunch of stuff along the guardrail. Thankfully, he seemed okay, although shaken up, and not getting up. I stopped to try and help, but there were already 2 other bikers on-scene, and they were already calling an ambulance as I was pulling up. 

I ended up talking to one of the guys who was helping out (Yamaguchi San), and talking about bikes for a bit. He was able to practice some English (He was quite good), and I was able to practice a bit of Japanese. It's really nice to talk to people and use a combination of Japanese and English, although I know that I am speaking in ~80% English.

## Mt. Kurokami

So many cool places that I passed, so I can't talk about everything, but one of the coolest places I ran into was Mt. Kurokami. I came across this as I was taken down a super narrow road, which ran into a massive reservoir, nestled in-between the mountains. The space was so cool, I didn't know what it was all about. I noticed a building overlooking the whole area, and so I wanted to check it out. 

I got to a T intersection, and there was a sign for "Entrance to Mt. Kurokami". I thought "Amazing!" so I took it, but quite quickly, I ran into a dead-end. Not great, so I had to turn around,but I got my action cam charged again and was able to take a little video. If I had more time, I might've explored it more, and after looking at Google maps (Which wasn't loading at the time), it looks like the observation deck was only a 5 minute walk up some stairs.

## Sasebo

I arrived in Sasebo around 6pm. It took me about another 45 minutes to find a hotel, and then another 30 minutes to find parking I am only semi-confident about. Sasebo is a really interesting city. My entire time in Japan, I've periodically seen some foreigners, but 95% of them were clearly just tourists. Here in Sasebo, since there's a large Naval base, there are a lot more Americans who are more ingrained in the local culture. In addition to that, it's much more of a "melting pot" style culture than anywhere else in Japan that I've been. There are authentic Mexican, German, American, Indian, and Italian places here, in almost equal quantities. These places exist elsewhere in Japan, but not in such an equal spread as what Sasebo has. 

I will probably explore Sasebo a bit more tomorrow morning, but it's been a really cool city to hang around this evening. I went to a cheap Ramen place on the corner for dinner, which had some great Tonkotsu ramen.
