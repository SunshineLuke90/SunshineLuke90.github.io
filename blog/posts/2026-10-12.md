# Japan Trip - Day 2?

The joke of it is, with the flight, I basically skipped a day. I left the US Sunday Morning, and arrived on Monday in the middle of the day, while it was still Sunday in the US. Weird feeling. But, I did get to spend the rest of the day walking around Tokyo, and resting at the hotel I stayed at.

## Getting through Security

Since I arrived in Tokyo around 1, I had about 2 hours to kill before I could check into the hotel, as it opened at 3. Most of the security went just fine, if taking a while to walk to (it was about 1.5 km from the terminal to security). However, I got stopped at the very end to get my bags & story checked out. My guess, a guy coming in with 2 bags, for 20 days, with 2 days worth of hotels booked, probably threw up a couple of flags. The guy at security liked my story though, and everything in my bags checked out, so he let me go pretty quickly.

Unfortunately, after getting through security, my plan was supposed to be to go to Iron Heart, which is a fancy denim store in Tokyo. However, it was closed! This was a big shock to my plans, which were initially to go to Iron Heart, then to go back to Tokyo city center, then to make it back to my hotel. So, I instead decided to go straight towards the hotel, and get some jeans at a motorcycle gear shop (Motomorida).

## Train to Shinbashi (新橋)

The first thing I had to do, was get a train to Shinbashi station, which was close to where my hotel would be. They do make it easy to get a ticket, but I'm just not smart enough to nail it on my first try. I ended up just going up to the counter, where the staff spoke fantastic english, and were able to get me a ticket to Shinbashi station. I ran into a couple of other Americans on the train platform, and was able to help them to get to where they were going (though not very confidently)

![Train ticket to Shimbashi from Haneda](/blog/images/ShinagawaTicket.JPG)

The train was definitely packed, but everyone got off at Shinagawa station (品川), which made it way easier for me to get space, given I've got my backpack, my helmet strapped to my backpack, and my duffel bag.

## Walking around Tokyo

Once I made it to Shinbashi, I started walking towards the motorcycle shop, which was super out of the way in an alley. Immediately I got my first big culture shock. Everyone walks on the left! Me being an American, I generally walk on the right side of the sidewalk, so walking on the right in Japan, I was constantly walking against the flow of traffic. Good thing I'm beginning to break this habit now, while walking, rather than as I'm on a motorcycle.

![A family taking a forklift for sports day](/blog/images/SportsDay.JPG)

The shop itself was quite interesting. They seemed more focused on motorcycle and car style gear, than on actual motorcycle gear. They did have a couple of helmets, but nothing fancy. I ended up finding a pair of jeans that fit, but it was labeled 34-32, which was odd given it was almost long on me, and I wear 34-34. Oh well. Apologies to my denim conscious friends, the John Doe jeans that I got probably aren't the same quality as the Iron heart jeans that I would've gotten. Hopefully I like these, and am able to get a pair of Iron Heart jeans on the way back.

## Checking in

![A pachinko parlor, right next to my hotel](/blog/images/PachinkoShinbashi.JPG)

By the time I had gotten my jeans, it was time to check into the hotel. As I got closer to the hotel, I quickly realized that I probably didn't book something in the "nicest" place in town. Getting into the hotel was my next culture shock, as I had to take my shoes off immediately as I walked in the door, and put them in a locker to even be able to enter the elevator, as the lockers had key fobs to enter through little turnstile gates. 

The check in process was smooth, as the lady at the front desk had fantastic english, but I hit a few stumbling blocks, fumbling around to find my locker, and also fumbling on how to make it to my floor. Not only that, but just the culture shock of guys walking around fully nude in the locker room, right next to the registration desk, was very odd. I imagine it would be the same in any public bath house, but this being my first, as I was also trying to get my reservation sorted, was a shock to say the least. 

I laid in bed for a little while, letting my phone recharge, and my eyes and legs rest for a bit. The jet lag honestly wasn't bad, but my eyes were a bit dry.

## Out shopping

![A street in Ginza, blocked off, probably for Sports Day](/blog/images/GinzaSportsDay.JPG)

Finally, I was able to ditch my duffel, and my helmet. My backpack had never felt so light. I decided to hit the few places I had planned on going to initially (Bic Camera, Onitsuka Tiger), so I headed out to Bic Camera first. 

Bic Camera was WILD. Absolutely packed with people, and 7 stories of everything electronic you could think of. Cameras, phones, watches, pens, video games, TVs, lights... everything. I had passed a few Seiko stores on the way to the Bic Camera, and had been looking for a nylon strap seiko for a while, and they had one! That was on my list of "things I can buy", so I went for it. I was also supposed to get a micro SD card for my camera, plus some extra batteries, but the SD card was SO expensive, so I didn't get one. The 1TB micro SD card I was loaned will have to do.

Exiting Bic Camera, I headed on to the Onitsuka Tiger store in the KITTE Marunouchi (KITTE丸の内) shopping mall. So many pairs to choose from, but I went with the black with white accents. Good price too!

## Bathing

The bath house was quite nice. Lucky that I didn't have tattoos too, since they prohibited them at this place. Learning how to shower sitting down was a bit of a trick, but honestly I kinda liked the process. Then, it was on to the bath, and sauna on the lower floor. Oh, multi-story bath house by the way. Saunas and cold plunges on the 2nd floor, hot baths and showers on the 3rd floor, and the locker room and registration on the 4th floor. That took me way too long to understand, even with a multi-story map. 

Using a public bath house for the first time was definitely a culture shock in itself, but it wasn't that bad since it was so normal. Hopefully I'll be going to fancier ones during my time in Kyushu.
