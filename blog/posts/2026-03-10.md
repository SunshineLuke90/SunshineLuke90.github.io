# Esri Dev & Tech Summit

This year was my first time attending one of the large Esri conferences. I had been to one of the Esri Midwest User Conferences before, but I have been diving more into actual software development and wouldn't be able to attend the UC because of it's proximity to the World Cup, so the Dev Summit seemed like the best option. Fantastic choice! I really enjoyed Palm Springs. The food is great, the weather is basically perfect, and the conference is large, but not so gigantic that it's a complete mess to get anywhere. If you're into GIS Software Development, or even just software development and have a passing interest in GIS, this is the place to go.

## Day -1

My time in Palm Springs was preceded by the travel to Palm Springs. This immediately got off to a rough start, as when I arrived at the Kansas City International Airport, it was entirely shut down due to a Bomb Threat. The entire airport was evacuated, and all departures and arrivals were completely halted. I ended up having to wait in a shuttle to get to the airport for about 2 hours.

Once I got into the airport, my flight was so delayed that the connection I was initially supposed to make just wasn't going to happen. I was supposed to leave out of MCI at 2:30, but ended up leaving around 4:30. I got into Denver around 6:00, and had to wait until 9:10 until the flight out to Palm Springs departed.

Arriving in Palm Springs, the airport was just completely showing off. Immediately after you left the boarding area for the terminal, you step **outside**, into a covered area, which is actually the secured terminal. For me, this was a first, and was completely foreign.

The uber ride to my hotel was fine, and the hotel room was also just fine. Not much to report.

## Day 0

This day, the only thing that was scheduled for the Dev & Tech summit was registration and a welcome party, at 5:30. I felt it would be a waste to not explore Palm Springs in the meantime, so I got up at a reasonable time, and went to a Coffee Shop called Townie Bagels. I got the "Sunnie Dunes" bagel with an everything bagel, which was quite good, but the standout feature was the cold brew, which was fantastic!

After breakfast, I went on a hike to Tahquitz Canyon trail, which was a 2 mile loop, going up a canyon with an active stream and waterfall. It was such a cool vibe, with a lot of flora and fauna, and the hike was moderate, but not strenuous. I really enjoyed it, and my Oura ring said it burned like 1100 calories, which was a fantastic added benefit.

I rested for a couple hours, and then went on to explore downtown Palm Springs. My uber driver the day before had said that the restaurants downtown were fantastic, so I made it a goal to explore the restaurants over the course of the week. I looked at most of the restaurants, but there was a Deli (Sherman's Deli & Bakery) that piqued my interest. I ended up getting a NY Roast Beef Sandwich, with a pistachio cannoli, which was also fantastic. One thing that caught me off guard with this place was that they brought out 3 huge pickle wedge slices. Bringing out 1 would already be a fun surprise, but 3?! Mania.

I explored the shops around the downtown area, but not having any luggage space really prevented me from picking anything up. Plus, I knew there would be Esri merch to pick up or buy at the conference, so I figured I'd wait until at least after day 1.

To cap off the day, I showed up to the Dev & Tech Summit registration desk (Entering through the opposite side than I was supposed to). I got checked in, and went to the registration party, where I met 2 of the Experience Builder Community greats, Jeffrey Thompson and Brian McLeer. I made it a point that I'd reach out to them again later in the conference to swap ideas within Experience Builder, to pitch ideas on widget standardization. I also ran into \_\_\_\_ from a startup called SewerAI. We chatted about our work history after college, and the troubles each of us had in our work. It seemed like we were headed in opposite directions in our career, but in the sort of way that we were both heading to a middle point, just from opposite sides.

## Day 1

The first day of the actual conference. Breakfast was alright (The parfait was fantastic, and the hot coffee was really good), and Jeff and Brian met me for breakfast as well. No shop talk this morning, but it was nice to get recognized by them, and have someone to talk to.

I bought an Esri hoodie at the merch booth, and went to the Plenary session (large, all conference session), which was mostly boring. The discussion about MCP server, and being able to create custom agents using Copilot Studio was VERY interesting. I had really low hopes for AI usage within GIS, since the Arcade assistant is pretty useless, and Microsoft Copilot is worse than useless. I'm not anti AI, I use it quite often while working on coding projects, but GeoAI as a drop in product with no configuration required doesn't seem like something that I have much hope for. Being able to fully configure an AI model for use across an organization was very interesting to me.

I met up with Adam Fackler, from NAPSG, and we got lunch together at a burger place called Tyler's Burgers. It's a really small place, but the food was very good. I got a patty melt, and Adam got 2 cheeseburger sliders (Which was totally the move). We ended up going to all of the afternoon sessions together, starting with an introductory session to Experience Builder Developer Edition.

I was hoping to pick up a couple of "best practices" that I may not have been applying during my use of the product. To my surprise, when they were going over using other's widgets in your Experience builder project, they used MY WIDGET as the example! Specifically, they used the [Slider Filter Widget](https://www.npmjs.com/package/exb-slider-filter). Not only did they show how to add the widget to their project, they completely installed it, and used a demo Experience Builder app (Which they showed off in the plenary session), and added my widget, fully configured it (With a data source, set up the filters, used the theming, everything), and showed it working. They had the PERFECT use case for it too, with multiple charts, and a map all redrawing when the filter was applied. They also gave me a shout-out, not knowing I was in the room watching them show off my widget.

I went up to the presenters afterwards, introducing myself as the developer of the custom widget they showed off, and I showed them the CLI that I had developed for Experience Builder. They were really impressed, and seemed genuinely interested in sharing what I'd built with the rest of the Experience Builder team, and maybe sharing what I'd built to the rest of the community, in a more official capacity. **Exciting stuff!**

The other 2 sessions I attended were less groundbreaking for me. There was one on the Javascript Maps SDK, which was cool, but not much I hadn't already used and worked on. The other was on building dashboards to support real-time operations, which was good, but didn't help a whole lot, since it was focused on ArcGIS Velocity, which is most useful for datasets that need to update faster than 1x per minute.

I did attend the welcome party at the end of the day, but the people I knew had already left, so I got some bread pudding and left.

## Day 2

The second day started similarly, got breakfast, which was consistent, good but not amazing. I was quickly getting used to the iced coffee being consistently available, and to have the fancy cucumber water in the water dispensers.

The plenary session was interesting, rather than catering to people brand new to the ArcGIS Ecosystem like day 1, it focused on some more specific technologies. I really liked the presentation on how to improve governance in an organization, using webhooks kicked off from various processes within the ArcGIS Portal ecosystem. It looks like right now everything is only available in ArcGIS Enterprise (Not in ArcGIS Online) but given the use cases for the tech, I would be very surprised if it doesn't come to ArcGIS Online at some time soon.

The presentation on Embeddings was really interesting. I could see how these embeddings could be used really well in damage assessment workflows.

Over the course of the day I went to a few sessions, primarily the ArcGIS Arcade session, and the ArcGIS SDK for Flutter. The guys at the Flutter presentation were really awesome, and helped me with every step of setting up the flutter project. I continued to come back to find them throughout the week, and they were able to help me set everything up, and even showed me a bunch of little shortcuts and features of VS Code that I didn't know (Like multiline comment shortcut ctrl+/). 

The Arcade presentation was pretty good too, since it showed off how Arcade expressions could be used for data validation (Attribute rules) in ArcGIS Pro, as well as showing off various "ignored" functions in Arcade that more people should use. I was doing pretty good on most of it, but the Decode function will save me a ton of heartache. 

## Day 3

The keynote session was fine, mostly user presentations of what various organizations had worked on. The Norwegian Air Ambulance presentation made me really jealous of an organization that was able to move quickly, and develop really complex systems. I think seeing that presentation, and how some custom development/tweaking can really improve a workflow, has pushed me more into the development side of things. The salesforce presentation was mostly boring, with the exception of the showcase of their custom mobile app, and speech recognition model. The workflow they described was a fieldworker doing inspections on streetlight poles, where the worker while walking to the pole hit a "start recording" button on their phone and then did a verbal inspection like "The pole seems okay, there's a bit of dry rotting on the bottom of the pole, but within margins, the bulb is getting a bit old, etc" and then when the worker hit stop recording, it sent the audio recording to salesforce's AI and it returned to completely fill out the survey that the fieldworker had, allowing them to edit things as needed. I could really see the use case for this, and if it's something that can be made more widely available, it will be used a LOT.

I spent a lot more of my time this day at the Tech zone, talking to various Esri developers. I also took some time to give feedback on web components to the Esri team. Most of my feedback was pretty positive, and I think my feedback was more coming from me not being an experienced developer until super recently, and not fully understanding how to use components and libraries. I got a really nice metal water bottle at the end of it, which was super cool!

There was an advanced Experience Builder session, which I knew most of the content for, but there were a couple of things that I didn't realize. One being that you don't need to use the your-extensions folder to build widgets in, you can actually use any folder you want, and it just needs to have a manifest.json file in it, to be recognized by webpack. Also seeing some of their GitHub repos where they have examples was really nice. For the most part, it validated my approaches to using GitHub for experience builder development. 

The vite session was really good. There was so much complexity that I didn't get most of it, so I will be coming back to the presentation to better understand it. The showcase of Sonda was super cool, being able to analyze everything that gets bundled with an application in a web interface was SO COOL. I would love to figure out how I could hopefully use this within Experience Builder, since those projects always seem to get super bloated for no reason.

This night was the big Developer Summit party, which included the Dodgeball tournament. I signed up for the Dodgeball tournament earlier that day, and when I got to the party, I ran into the members of my team, all of which were young, tall guys. They all reminded me of my girlfriend Ryann's brother Jackson. We were feeling super confident, and we all got along super well immediately. Once we actually made it onto the Dodgeball court, things fell apart pretty quickly... Turns out, being strong, tall, and young doesn't actually help, it just makes you a bigger target. We lost 2 in a row, embarrassingly quickly. We got a couple of beers afterwards (I got the 805 blonde ale, which was honestly really good.) and I grabbed a rice and chicken bowl after a bit. I ended up eating at a table, and an Esri employee (Who will remain unnamed) came up and chatted with me. He was really nice, and we talked about things that we had been working on, and my future career aspirations as a GIS software developer. In some ways, he talked me out of moving into a role as a software developer at Esri, since many of the challenges being solved at Esri don't really have anything to do with creating cool GIS stuff, they're more software engineering focused. Instead, he pointed me towards companies that are more GIS users, rather than producers, which I am actually leaning more towards now. We must've talked for like an hour and a half, he was so kind.

## Day 4

This was the final day of the Developer Summit, and there were only a couple of sessions planned for the morning. I ended up going to a Git presentation, on how to collaborate between developers using Git (Which I am very familiar with). Everything they went over seemed quite obvious to me, but it was good to hear how they presented things, translating Git terminology into more plain english, and showing examples through a whole project. I ended up asking a few questions about how to improve Git adoption, and it was refreshing to hear everyone's surprise that my organization wasn't using Git at all for code storage/collaboration.

The last real session of the conference was on custom printing reports via ArcGIS Enterprise, which was really interesting and useful. I haven't had a chance to dive into custom printing reports, but after having the presentation recorded, it will be something that I look into.

The closing session was by far the most informal session of the whole conference, focused more on feedback and just wrapping up things, rather than clean professional presentations. I kinda liked the more simple nature of the closing presentation, and it was nice to see a more unfiltered side of the presenters who normally sound like they're reading a teleprompter.

The day ended early, around noon, so I went back to my hotel, and spent most of the rest of the day chilling by the pool, which was very refreshing. 

## Closing 

Overall, the Developer Summit was by far the best conference I've been to, and has made me very excited to go again. I learned so much, and feel like a much better developer for it. Making contacts, and taking a solo trip to California (First time by the way) was really refreshing, and made me feel like a more confident professional, rather than feeling like the new kid on the block. If you're someone who is interested in getting more into software development, and you've been using the ArcGIS OOTB software suite for a while, I highly recommend going to the Developer Summit, as they have sessions for a wide range of professionals.
