# Japan Trip - Day 16

I got a bit of a later start today. Nothing terrible. The goal for today was to visit Shibuya, and to make it to the Sega store for some Shadow themed souvenirs for my girlfriend. Beyond that, no plan, just explore. I know Shibuya has a lot of super popular stuff around it, so I was sure I'd have plenty to do and see. 

I left the hotel, and wanted to return to that cafe that I visited on the 24th. Yet again, as I walked past, there was a line of tourists out the door. I have no idea how I was able to go straight in the first time, it's been packed every other time I've walked past. But, unlike the day prior, I was quite hungry, so I stopped at the family mart on the corner, and picked up a monster, donut, and a couple of onigiri for the road.

When I walked into the Ueno station today, there was a JR popup shop, with a bunch of JR merch. I looked at all of it, so cute, but nothing quite stuck out enough to me to secure a purchase out of me. The Yamanote line t-shirt was really sick though. I really thought about it. If I think about it too much more, it might get me tomorrow.

## Shibuya station

I took the train to Shibuya station, and somehow I got a train that dropped me off at a relatively easy exit. Basically only one path to get out of the station. I've heard that Shibuya station was one of the most challenging stations in Japan, but it was quite easy for my specific train. After doing some research online, it looks like Shinjuku is the most complex/busy station in Tokyo. I might end up going there tomorrow, to see if I will get confused. 

My first destination was still the Sega store, but along the way, I ran across the famous Shibuya scramble crossing. It was cool that there were a lot of digital signboards around the square, but honestly it was a bit underwhelming. 

![A picture, across Shibuya Crossing](/blog/images/shibuyaCrossing.JPG)

There were some other tourists who would run out in the middle of the crossing when all of the lights were red but the crosswalk hadn't turned green yet, and it honestly pissed me off. Inside of myself I was like "You don't walk until the sign goes green" even though in America, I almost always start walking before the walk sign changes. There were a lot of people taking pictures in the middle of the crossing too, and people taking videos. Honestly, not a good photo spot, and very annoying and over the top. There are plenty of fantastic photo-op spots across Tokyo, and even multiple in Shibuya, with a great one in the scramble square. The Hachiko statue (famous dog who waited for his owner to return) is in the scramble square, and has a fantastic background for a photo. Why not use that?!

## Shibuya Parco

The Sega store was in a much larger building (after research, Shibuya Parco) which had a bunch of fashion brands on the bottom, cafes, and popup throughout. The 6th floor was wild though, fully gaming themed. It included a Nintendo store, the Sega store, a Godzilla store, a Pokémon store, a Jump store (Shonen), and a couple others. I was really just there for the Sega store, but I also picked up a couple of game controller keychains from the Nintendo store, and some Pokémon stuff for my parents.

While I was in the Pokémon store, I called my parents, since I couldn't remember exactly what Pokémon they were looking for, and I figured giving them the ability to see what they might get would help. Luckily they were both together, so I was able to video call my Mom and we picked out some good pieces.

After making it through the Pokémon store, I stayed on the call and did a little debrief of my trip so far, talking about the differences I've noticed between Tokyo and Kyushu. One of the big things that I've noticed is the disparity between foreigners and locals. In Sasebo, I felt the culture there was very much a melting pot culture. It felt like the local vibe was very much that of integration, and that the American foreigners were being intentional to meet and mingle with the Japanese population, and that has created a culture where all populations feel more approachable. Obviously this is advanced by the US naval base in Sasebo, which I'm sure makes it so that there is a constant dominant US presence. However, the vibe in Tokyo is significantly different, from both sides. 

The foreign population seems significantly less interested with meeting and conversing with the local population. I think that comes 70% from the foreigners being tourists, who just want to see the sights, and 30% from the locals having seen so many foreigners that the vibe from the foreigners perspective is that the locals won't really be interested, won't have time, or the language barrier. But, it's not just the foreigners end of things. Since there are so many foreigners, I imagine the local population here has both grown tired and gotten used to the foreign population. This means that when they see a foreigner, there's no excitement or interest in why they're there, or what they're doing. It's obvious what they're doing in Tokyo, they're seeing Tokyo. 

I'm not trying to draw any sort of "good" or "bad" here. I don't think there's necessarily anything inherently wrong with the local population not being interested in talking to foreigners, it's just natural. That being said, my goal for this trip was to meet people, and have emergent interactions. If I had been looking for that in Tokyo, I would be hard pressed to have this sort of interaction.

Anyway, rant over. 

I left the Shibuya Parco building with my newly gathered merch, packed between my backpack and the massive shopping tote that I got from the Sega store. No destination in mind, I just wanted to walk around Shibuya some more. 

## Tower Records

As I was walking around, I found a Tower Records! I always love record stores, and I know Ryann had talked about a Tower Records in California a while ago, so I figured I should check it out. OH BOY am I glad I did. Immediately, I ran into some of my favorite Japanese albums. I found the Kessoku Band album (From Bocchi the Rock, an anime) as well as all of the small cover albums and singles. I also immediately found some of the best Japanese Jazz albums, right around the corner from the Bocchi the Rock album, including Scenery, Funky Stuff, and Casiopea. Not only the classics, but they included a Zutomayo record, and even an Inabakumori album! I have not met a single person IRL who has even heard of either Zutomayo or Inabakumori, so to see these albums in the "Featured" section was WILD to me. 

![A picture of some CDs on a table](/blog/images/shibuyaCDs.JPG)

I picked up all of the highlights, and decided to head upstairs to really check out the catalog, as the place had like 7 floors. One thing I found odd, most of the music was on CDs, not Vinyl. This was super weird to me, since in America, almost all of the physical media nowadays is sold on Vinyl, with a little bit of stuff on CD or Cassette. Here, there was still a Vinyl floor, and there were definitely Vinyl records around, but the primary physical media was clearly CDs. 

Upstairs, I found a couple of other really solid albums. Namely, Geography by Tom Misch. I have been looking for this album on physical media for SO LONG. It's an album that got me through a lot, and means a lot to me for a lot of reasons. An added bonus, the title of the album is Geography, which is pretty nice. Geography was the only album I picked up today that was in English though, I figured if I want to add something like Radiohead to my collection, I will have no problem finding it in America, but if I'm looking for Zutomayo, there's absolutely no shot I find it ever again.

## Shimokitazawa

After leaving Tower Records, I was in a very "Music" mood. Later this week, I'm planning on attending a live event in Shimokitazawa, both because I want to see some live music in Japan, and also because Shimokitazawa is heavily featured in the Bocchi the Rock anime, which focuses on an introverted high school girl who joins a band, and goes through the trials and tribulations of playing in a small band. The whole anime is centered in Shimokitazawa, and the event venue they play in is actually a real place! 

![A picture of the stairway down to Shelter, a live music venue in Shimokitazawa](/blog/images/shimokitazawaShelter.JPG)

I went back to the Shibuya scramble crossing, which is also where many of Shibuya station's exits spit out to, and took a train to Shimokitazawa. It was a much more local line, and the tourist population had almost entirely vanished as I got on the train. What a relief! In Shimokitazawa, I was there to see 3 places: the venue I would be seeing the live music, the venue from the Bocchi the Rock anime, and Village Vanguard. 

I started with the venue. I made my way there, and along the way the shops that I passed were a lot different than in Shibuya or Ginza, since the shops seemed much more local, and had a much more punk/grunge vibe. Shimokitazawa still has some large buildings, but nothing on the scale as the skyscrapers that you'd find in central Tokyo or Shibuya. This all contributed to a much more "Vibey" space, and paired with a lot more music themed art and decorations, I really liked the space.

I found the venue, but didn't go in. There was a guy sitting on the stairs just past the venue, and I didn't think I'd be very welcome coming in with my backpack and my massive sonic themed shopping bag. Figured it was just best to know how far away it was, and where I'd need to be going later that week.

Next stop was Village Vanguard. It was a super all over the place shop, but everything was very focused on music, pop culture, and local artists. There were little popup audio listening spots all around, where you could hear snippets of an album before purchasing. Super cool. They actually had a couple of Indigo Jam Unit albums just sitting there, which was CRAZY because I knew Indigio Jam Unit from the one song they have on Spotify, which is only on Spotify because it's part of a compilation album, and I was looking for it at Tower Records but couldn't find any of their music. So to find not one but TWO of their albums here was a solid sign that they had some underground stuff.  

I continued on, and went to visit the Shelter lounge, where Bocchi the Rock is set. In the anime, the place is called "Starry". Oh, by the way, the whole day it was raining. Like nonstop. Shelter is a fairly popular venue, and this month they're doing a sort of anniversary celebration. They actually have Tricot playing there on the 29th, but it's sold out. I'm so jealous.

After visiting the Shelter lounge, I was basically ready for the next location, so I started walking back to the station. But, out of the corner of my eye, I caught a sign for a record shop... or, was it a cafe... Wait, it's both!? There was a tiny sign on the side of this building, for a cafe which had a record player at each table. Looked sick, so I hopped in an elevator (The only way down) and went to the basement of this building, which spit me out directly next to the cafe bar. What a vibe! The cafe bar was really cool, and they had a small but solid menu. The listening stations were also almost completely full when I went down, save for a single station at the back of the store, so I was super lucky to have a spot. 

![A picture of the record station, and the cappuccino and pudding affogato](/blog/images/shimokitazawaCafeStation.JPG)

They walked me through the whole process, I even signed up for a punch card on Line, to save a couple hundred yen on the seat charge. They had a whole 5 minute long video that they had me watch to make sure I knew the procedures for how to handle records, and the cafe limitations. 

### Accessibility tangent

A side tangent that I want to take here, I think one of the things that Japan has completely solved is tourist accessibility, and they're working on solving accessibility at large as well. This extends to so many things. The rumble strips and walking cane guide paths are EVERYWHERE, street crossings make chirping or bell sounds depending on the type of crosswalk, and they're projected from the opposite side of the crosswalk. Almost every piece of Japanese text has english translation directly below it (By default) and almost always you'll find Korean and Chinese translations close by, and there are alternate options for just about every type of menu, video, guidance, map, etc. It's made my trip infinitely easier, and when I think about the US, while Spanish does exist, and significantly more in border states, I can't really think of any signage, menu or museum text translation that just exists by default, side by side with the English translation. I really commend Japan for making it so easy for tourists, and for having so many accessibility accommodations throughout their society.

Anyways, back to the cafe.

![A picture of headphones, and a lamp with halloween decorations in the background](/blog/images/shimokitazawaCafeLamp.JPG)

I ordered a cappuccino, and a pudding affogato. The cappuccino was quite good, and the pudding affogato came out in almost a martini glass, with a jello like pudding, a scoop of vanilla ice cream, and a shot of espresso served alongside. The seat only came with 90 minutes, and you were only allowed to take 3 records at a time back to your desk, so I grabbed a Radwimps album, a Tatsuro Yamashita album, and the Kessoku Band album (for peak vibes) back to my station. What a vibe! I listened to the first 2 albums all the way through, sipping on my cappuccino and enjoying the pudding affogato along the way. I closed it out with the highlights from Kessoku Band, and in total that took about 90 minutes. Such a cool vibey cafe, and I think I will probably show up again later this week before attending the live event, since I'll be back in Shimokitazawa.
