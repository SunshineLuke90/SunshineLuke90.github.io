# Japan Trip - Day 18

This morning started a bit earlier than usual. I had planned on playing a video game "Big Walk" with some of my friends back in the US, but I needed a pc to play on. I was actually able to find an eSports cafe in Shinjuku, and I left the hotel around 7:50 to be able to make it there around 8:30, to install the game before 9.

I left the hotel at a good time, and stopped at Family Mart before entering the train station. I picked up a Monster, a couple of Onigiri, and a pancake pastry, to eat when I got to the eSports cafe. The train ride was super simple, I've got it down at this point for the basic rides, and I wasn't actually going to the real Shinjuku station, but one just before, so even getting out at the destination was a breeze. Finding the eSports cafe was a bit of a challenge, since it was on the 8th floor of a building, and I guess was in an eSports area, because there were 2 or 3 other eSports cafes in the exact same area that I almost went to.

The eSports cafe was exactly what I was looking for. High quality PC, mid quality peripherals, and a fantastic wired LAN connection. The environment was super nice, most people were playing either Valorant or League, but mostly Valorant. I couldn't figure out how to change the OS language to English, but I was able to use google translate on my phone, and my memory, to figure the Steam UI out enough to log in, and download and launch the game. I got everything figured out on time to have the game going before 9, and we played for about an hour and a half. No issues whatsoever, it's so cool to be able to play games together, keeping connected in real time on opposite sides of the earth.

I stayed at the cafe for another hour or two, talked to my girlfriend, and played some League of Legends. It was a really cool experience to be sitting in a cafe with a bunch of people who were also into playing video games. It can be a bit of an isolating experience to play PC games typically, since you're usually relegated to your own house, and it's not really a portable setup you can bring to a friends house. You're almost always isolated when playing games, which made it extra cool to be around a bunch of other gamers in person, even if we weren't playing together, or even playing the same game. Just having that physical presence and community was a really cool thing. I would love it if America had eSports cafes, but I haven't really seen them.

## Lunch

If I'm being honest, I haven't really had any sushi on my trip. It's not like I've been avoiding sushi, it's just that other foods have been more common, and more popular, so I've been going with what's common and popular. That being said, I was running up against the end of the trip, so I decided to try a Kura sushi to be able to compare to back home. I found one nearby the eSports cafe, on the 3rd floor of a building. I was able to seat myself, but instead of it being a conveyor cafe, the conveyor was down, and you had to order all items for individual order. A bit unfortunate, but no problem.

The sushi itself was honestly kinda mid. About on par with the sushi I'd had in KC. That being said, there were a LOT more options for sushi & sashimi here in Japan, and the dessert options were better too. I was happy with the meal, but I wouldn't say it was anything to write home about. I got a cool shaved ice, and a matcha ice cream that was really good.

## Pre-Concert Prep

I returned to my hotel, since it was around 2pm at this point, and I needed to do laundry before the concert, which started at 6:30PM. I got the laundry started, and got some rest in the hotel while waiting. When I came down for the dryer though, there were a couple of issues. Firstly, I forgot my room key, so I would need to talk to the front desk for help, and second, the dryer didn't fully dry the clothes. So annoying. 

I stuck more money into the dryer, and got the key figured out, but I didn't start the laundry as soon as I got back to the hotel, so I was getting really worried I'd be late to the concert and would get bumped. After 20 more minutes of drying, I went back to check and the clothes still weren't dry, but I needed to make it to my concert so I stuffed my half-dry clothes in my duffel, and took them up to my room and draped anything even slightly damp all over my micro room. As soon as I did that, I headed out, and then quickly realized I forgot my earplugs and had to rush back to the hotel to grab them, but then headed out for Chikamichi, the venue I was seeing, which is in Shimokitazawa. 

## Chikamichi

![The wall of the upstairs of the venue, with lots of tour posters on the glass](/blog/images/chikamichiPosters.JPG)

When I got to the venue, it was a really cool place, with a ton of band posters on the wall, for various tours, and for venues and the venue schedules. I paid for my ticket, and got a drink ticket, and headed outside and downstairs to the venue. I had arrived a bit late, so there was already a band playing, and they were super young. Like they looked like they were still in high school. But even so, their music was pretty good, and their energy was massive. They only played one or two songs before they left, and the next band began setting up. 

![The stage of Chikamichi, with blue lights covering the stage](/blog/images/chikamichiStage.JPG)

I won't describe every band here, but my favorite band of the night was トトトト (pronounced totototo). They had such a good energy and vibe, and their music had some really technical guitar. I think one of my favorite parts about Japanese rock/indie music is that the guitar and bass have a significantly higher technicality baseline than American rock/indie. It makes for really interesting instrumental listening, since the lead guitar is often flying, and the bass is always walking around and adding extra flourishes between the changes. Add to all of that the fact that Japan has a slightly different default pop chord progression, and you get some really interesting music, even if the band is super small or young. All of the artists who played tonight were fantastic, and I had a fantastic time.

After the concert, I stuck around for a little bit, to try and talk to some of the bands and tell them how good they sounded. The crowd was pretty small, and it seems like the Japanese crowds typically don't really get wild or excited, so I wanted to ensure they knew how good they sounded. I was able to talk to トトトト, and congratulate them on their performance, and they seemed really interested in why I was there. We went through some of the basics, like where I am from, why I came to Japan, and why Chikamichi, and what I've been doing on my vacation. They were super nice, and I really wish I could've asked them more questions, but I just don't have the Japanese to make it happen, and they knew very little english. They seemed to like talking to me though, since their band took a photo with me! It's always really cool to get photos with locals, unprompted. 

![A photo with the band トトトト](/blog/images/chikamichiToToToTo.jpg)

After the concert, I headed back to the hotel, and basically crashed as soon as I got back. What a day!
