# Japan Trip - Day 6

Today started in Nagasaki. I had a pretty solid breakfast at the hotel, but I couldn't tell if it was Japanese style or Chinese style. The plate was fun, split into 9 little squares for you to put each of the little foods on. Obviously I had to get the curry Tonkotsu (probably my favorite dish), but I tried a smattering of other foods along the way.

## On the road

I set out pretty early, around 9 AM, since I thought today would need to be a longer journey. I had set the route the night before to make the first third pretty quick, the second third super windy, and the last third pretty quick again, that way I could get in the fun riding in the mountains, and still be able to make it from point A to point B with time to spare.

The start of the journey took me through a bunch of tunnels, and was on mostly open and straight road. Then, all of a sudden, my navigation had me take a couple of turns, and the road changed completely. It became basically a one-lane road, with moss on the base of the road, and nothing but dense forest on either side of the road for as far as I could see. 

![A motorcycle, on a narrow road, in a dense forest](/blog/images/forestMotoRoad.JPG)

It's hard to describe how majestic this section of road was, but the best I can compare it to is like you're hiking the Appalachian Trail in Tennessee, with trees all around, but you're on a motorcycle with a paved road, not hiking on rocks. Literally everything else is the same though. Probably in my top 5 favorite experiences is driving/biking on a road with trees fully covering you on all sides, and this was #1 within this experience. A truly magical road.

That isn't to say that there weren't some good views as well. On the same road, there were a couple of overlooks, looking out across a valley in the mountain. Everything here was beautiful, and I would later realize that this road was effectively the entrance to the Unzen-Amakusa National park.

I've been cataloguing everything in my trip in this Google Earth project, including planned and actual routes.

## Iwado shrine

The main destination that I wanted to hit on the route was the Iwado shrine, which is a really cool shrine, hidden in the mountains, and placed in a hole cut out of a mountain. Everything leading up to the shrine looked SO old, and the trees surrounding the shrine were huge, and had to be hundreds if not thousands of years old. 

![A picture of the Torii gate leading up to Iwado shrine](/blog/images/iwadoTorii.JPG)

When I arrived at the shrine on my bike, I found a parking lot ~300 meters from the shrine entrance. I pulled in, but I couldn't tell if it was the right parking lot for the shrine. After about a minute of fretting, and trying to translate a sign that was in the parking lot, another car pulled into the parking lot with 2 ladies in it. They got out pretty quickly, so I asked them if this was the parking lot for the Iwado shrine. (Really, I said: すみません、私は岩ど、parking, 大丈夫ですか？) They got the message, and reassured me that they were also going to Iwado shrine (shrine - 神社). 

On the walk up to the shrine, we crossed paths a few times. I got sidetracked at a stream, so they passed me, then they got sidetracked reading a sign that was 100% Japanese, so I passed them, and so on. We reached the shrine at about the same time, and it was so pretty. The trees surrounding the shrine were just as pretty as google showed, and the shrine itself had a really crazy presence to it.

![The Iwado shrine, backed by a stone cliff side](/blog/images/iwadoShrine.JPG)

## Ferry to Amakusa

Today I was headed for the city of Amakusa, which requires a ferry to get there from Nagasaki. Getting to the ferry was super easy, just pulled right into line, and the attendant knew english too! So easy to figure out where to go, and I was able to get my ticket and get back to line just as the ferry was pulling in to dock. The ride was super peaceful, only 30 minutes, and was a really nice relaxing break from the motorcycle all day.

Once I landed, I still had ~15 minutes of riding to make it to the city of Amakusa, but it was fairly uneventful as I simply had to follow the same road I got on, all the way to Amakusa.

## Amakusa nightlife

I checked into the hotel, and got a little bit of rest, just laying down. I decided I wanted to go to a more social food place tonight, rather than the easy but individualistic ramen joints that I had been going to. In Japan, there is a restaurant known as an Izakaya, which basically means it's a bar, that also serves food. This kind of place commonly has semi-private sectioned off rooms, as well as a bar area. I found a place in Amakusa, not too far from my hotel, called Yomiya, which was very highly reviewed, so I put on my new hoodie and headed out.

When I arrived, unless you read japanese, you couldn't tell that it was a restaurant outside of the noise coming from the front door. It was just a plain door with some split sheets in front of the door, in the middle of a neighborhood. I was so close to just walking away out of fear of the chance that I'd be walking into someone's house, but I am so glad that I didn't.

I walked in, and was immediately surprised at how busy the place was! There was only one seat left open at the bar, so I asked the man sitting next to the open seat if it was available, and sat down. Not only was this the first place that I had been to that was social, but it was also the first place with absolutely no english whatsoever. The menu was fully Japanese (And vertical text) and it didn't seem like I'd have much luck trying english with the restaurant staff. 

The couple sitting next to me seemed friendly, so I decided to ask them for help with choosing something from the menu, telling them that this was my first time at an Izakaya, and that I couldn't read the menu and didn't know how to order. Immediately, they were super friendly, and started helping me to read and asked what kinds of food I did and didn't like so that they could help me order. I was able to order a single skewer of pork with their help, but I didn't realize how small a portion that was, so I had to enlist their help to order more. By the time I had done that though, we had already been talking for 45 minutes, about Anime, sightseeing, America, and all kinds of things. 

This family was by far the highlight of my day, and without me noticing, they had also ordered some dishes on my behalf. They ordered some Yellowtail, in a format that I had never seen before, as well as some Japanese rice, which looked like the end of an eggroll. We continued talking, and we ended up talking for about 2.5 hours. When it was time to go, they even paid for my whole meal, including my drinks, which was far too kind, but they wouldn't allow me to pay. They said, it was a once-in-a-lifetime experience, and they couldn't allow me to pay. Truly, meeting their family has been the highlight of my trip so far.
