# Japan Trip - Day 3

Honestly a great nights sleep. One thing I've noticed though, the average room temperature everywhere in Japan is a few degrees higher than the average in America. Not sure why that is, maybe just used to a hotter climate?

I left the hotel around 8:30AM. Walked around for a bit, slowly moving towards Shinbashi station, but with no urgency, since today is all about just getting to Fukuoka, so that I can pick up the bike on the 14th.

I needed some food for the trip, so I got a Tuna Mayo Onigiri, Iced Latte, and what I thought was a water. Turns out, Pocari Sweat is more akin to a gatorade, even though it looks like a clear bottle of water. Good, but not what I was expecting. I had heard that it is bad manners (or just not really the norm) to eat food while standing or walking, so I stashed the Onigiri and Pocari Sweat in my duffel, and continued on my way.

![An Iced latte, Tuna Mayo Onigiri, and Pocari Sweat](/blog/images/Oct13Breakfast.JPG)

## Train Shenanigans

This being my second train, I was feeling confident that I would be able to figure out how to use one of the wall terminals on my own. I took about 3 shots at it to get it right.

- First try: Went straight to the Shinkansen booth, but realized I would still need a smaller transfer ticket first. 
- Second try: Went to the correct booth for the small train, but my card wasn't accepted. Backed off to prevent getting yelled at for being a dumb foreigner
- Third try: Grabbed a 1000 yen note out of my bag, tried same process, worked like a charm. 

Got on the train to Shinagawa station, easy. Got off at Shinagawa,and immediately found where to go for the Shinkansen. However, given my earlier frustrations with the Shinkansen machine, and how expensive the Shinkansen tickets are compared to the normal tickets, I wanted to have a human help me out with it. Found a person right away, and they very kindly helped me to figure out what I needed, and I made it onto the very next Shinkansen in the nick of time. 

## Hotel & Food

I hadn't really eaten anything since arriving in Japan, besides a single tuna mayo Onigiri. I walked to the hotel I was staying at (The Millenials Fukuoka), and thankfully they allowed me to check my bags with them so I could walk around. I was really hungry at this point, and I had been craving some curry rice with tonkotsu pork. Luckily, there was a place just down the street from my hotel that had just what I needed!

![Curry rice with tonkotsu pork and an egg](/blog/images/curryRice.JPG)

After getting the curry rice, I wanted to keep walking around and exploring, but it quickly started raining. "No big deal" I thought, but then it started POURING, so I had to rush into a Family Mart and buy an umbrella, which ended up being very small. This got me back to the hotel though, and I was able to check in completely, and I brought all of my stuff up to my room. 

I have heard that one of the reasons that you get extra tired when traveling is that your brain has to process all of the places you see. When you're driving or traveling somewhere you know well, your brain is able to fill in from memory, similar to a computer hitting it's cache. However, when you travel somewhere new, it's like every thing you see is a cache miss, which gets taxing quickly. This is exactly how I felt after being on the Shinkansen all day and looking out the window at all of the new things. It was only ~6pm, but I decided to call it a night and get to sleep.

Talking about sleep, it was another "capsule" hotel situation, I chose it because it was cheap. However, the bed was super fancy! It reclined, and the pod had an old I-Pod touch plugged in at the bed with a custom UI just for managing the bed. It even had it's own new user onboarding, where it had you turn the lights on, set your bed recline angle, and even set an alarm for the morning, where the bed would automatically recline in the morning when your alarm went off. One of the craziest things I've seen the whole trip.
