# Japan Trip - Day 17

Today, I didn't have much of an idea of what to do, besides that I wanted to go to Shinjuku station, since I had been having a god time traveling by train & subway, and wanted to see what "the final boss" would be like. 

## Breakfast

So, first thing, looked for breakfast. Like usual, I walked past the cafe near my hotel, and this time, no line! I think it also helped that I was getting quite a late start to the day, as I walked past the cafe around 10:40. I went in, and this time I got the egg & ham sandwich. It was really good, and came in 4 different slices, like the sandwich was split on it's diagonal twice. The coffee was still really good as well, and this time I took a couple of the sugar pieces as candies. 

![A cheesecake, with a fruit jam topping](/blog/images/uenoCafeCheesecake.JPG)

The meal also came with a dessert option, so I got the cheesecake. It was also quite good, the sweetness was a bit understated, which is fairly common throughout Japan, which I really like. I enjoy eating stuff that isn't just straight sugar, but allows for the rest of the ingredients to have a place. 

## Shinjuku station #1

I headed out to Shinjuku station after breakfast, and I was recommended to take the Yamanote line there. The Yamanote line is such a common route that I've been taking, and I think it definitely helps that the line is a full loop, so you can just kinda hop on and get a guaranteed train pretty quick, in either direction. Your maximum time on the train is only going to be like 30 minutes for any possible trip. When I arrived at Shinjuku station, the train was super easy to get off of, and there was basically only one obvious route to take. Once I took it, I was able to get out of the station almost immediately, with no fuss. What's so hard about this station? Final boss my butt. 

I didn't have much of a plan, but I knew I needed a suitcase at some point before the 29th, and I also could use some more clothes. Maybe another pair of jeans, and a shirt or two? Uniqlo has been my go-to store here for clothes, it's inexpensive, solid quality, and fits my style really well. The jeans that I got earlier in the week have been pretty solid, which is a huge deal for me since I've historically hated wearing jeans. 

When I exited the station, there was a park north of the station, so I decided to walk to the park as my first action. The park had a really cool mini soccer field, which was netted, and even had benches. I really wish KC had something like this in some of the downtown areas. Playing small field soccer, with nets to prevent having to chase down OOB balls is so nice. Plus, with nets you can then put the field right next to urban infrastructure with less worry of balls going everywhere and breaking stuff. KC if you're listening, put in some mini soccer fields. 

![A quick snap of the mini soccer field in the Shinjuku park](/blog/images/shinjukuSoccer.JPG)

I found a seat within the park, as there was a great viewing spot, where a lot of businessmen were already sitting. It was quite a nice view through the city, down one of the major roads in Shinjuku, by the government buildings. THe park itself was quite nice, a lot of good walking trails, but it wasn't like some of the other parks in Tokyo where I felt completely isolated from the city. It felt a lot closer to some of the parks in Chicago, where you're still definitely in the city, just also in a park in the city. Nothing bad about that, just a different scale to the park. 

![The view from the lookout spot in Shinjuku park](/blog/images/shinjukuParkView.JPG)

After I had sat in the park for a while, I decided to head back to Shinjuku, and headed for the more "Shoppy" area of Shinjuku, on the other side of the station. I headed for Uniqlo, but that wasn't as simple a task as I realized, since there were like 4 or 5 different Uniqlo shops within this area of Shinjuku. This is wild to me, since in America, the closest Uniqlo to KC is in Chicago. Gotta get what I can here! I intended on going to the Uniqlo flagship store in Shinjuku, but when I saw a Uniqlo sign, I went in the building, and it ended up being one of the smaller ones in the area. I didn't know that until I couldn't find a "Tax free"  counter, so I put the jeans that I was trying on back, and left to go to the bigger store, hoping they'd have a wider selection. 

The one big thing I was looking for was a pair of Slim Straight Jeans, in the Selvedge line. The previous store didn't have any normal Slim Straight Jeans, but I figured the flagship store had a better chance. When I walked in, there was a florist on the bottom floor. The shop was only medium sized, 3 floors, with a dedicated mens floor. I went up, and looked in the jeans section, but I couldn't find any straight slim selvedge, save for a display pair hanging in front of the racks. I asked one of the workers where the rest of the selvedge would be, and he said "last one". No way. The last pair was the display unit, which was a 28 waist (Never happening). Annoying, but I ended up going with another pair of the straight selvedge jeans, and a pair of their EZY jeans which felt like sweatpants. 

After stopping at Uniqlo, I wandered around the shops for a good while. Sometime in my wandering though, I stopped being able to send messages. I thought it was a bit odd, but I was in a super populated area, so I figured my phone was just being slow. I kept wandering, and found some cool niche shops, but nothing super out of the ordinary. I got tired at some point and sat on a bench outside, to check how to get back to my hotel in Ueno, but when I tried to search on google, it wouldn't load anything. Actually, nothing was loading, no google, no instagram, no spotify. It was at this point, I realized that the data plan that I bought for the trip was only a 15 day unlimited plan, not a 30 day plan. Whoops! 

This meant, for my second round in Shinjuku station, I would need to run it blind. No google maps assistance telling me exactly which entrance to go into, and exactly which platform I would need to take, just straight reading the signs and figuring it out. What a challenge!

## Shinjuku station #2

This time, I'm running blind. I just have to make it to Ueno, so shouldn't be too hard right? But this time, I'm in a much different place than I was, and I'll need to find the right line. I entered through a random entrance, as the entrances are EVERYWHERE and ended up in what felt like a shopping mall. It was like a whole extra city underground in the subway line, with all of the restaurants, clothing stores, etc. 

I was on a mission though. Make it back to Ueno and my hotel, so that I can get back on Wi-Fi and re-up my plan. Yes, I could go into a cafe or something and get free wifi there, but that's no fun. I want to put myself to the test. I continued walking through the station for what felt like 15 minutes, just following signs for the line that I needed to take, and going through twists, turns and all kinds of stuff. Finally, I emerged at a station platform, and was able to take the line I needed back to Ueno. 

I bought more data for my phone, so that I wouldn't have this issue again (as fun as it was), and rested for a bit. That night, I decided to go out in Ueno to try and find a suitcase for the return trip, and maybe get some food on the way. I ended up going to a McDonalds, since I hadn't been to one on my trip yet, and that was one of my goals, to experience a true foreign McDonalds.

## Out for the night

I got a Bai Spicy ZakuZaku Juicy Makku set. What a mouthful. Basically, it's a double patty chicken burger, with a spicy sauce. It came with fries, which I also got some "shaka shaka" seasoning for, and I upgraded the drink to a grape fanta float. What an odd order combination, but it ensured that every item would be unique, which was basically the goal. The shaka shaka seasoning is basically a tiny bag of seasoning, that comes with a much larger bag. You take your fries, dump them in the larger bag, then dump the bag of seasoning on the fries and... "shaka shaka". The seasoning is super cheap, and pretty good. I'm honestly surprised that something similar doesn't exist in the US. One of the huge benefits that I'd see with the seasoned fries is that they add some extra flavor, and make it so the fries need less sauce, potentially reducing extra sugar & calories. That being said, you'd still be eating at McDonalds, so how much extra calories through sauces matters is a bit of a moot point. 

After finishing my dinner, I walked around more in the Ueno strip, and ran into a resale shop which had some vintage video games. They actually had an Orange GameCube, which is exclusive to the Japan market, and one of my old coworkers told me to keep an eye out for it. Caleb, if you're reading this, if you message me before I leave I'll pick it up for you!

I then began looking for a suitcase. I came very close to buying one earlier this day in Shinjuku, but I am so glad that I didn't. The suitcase I was looking at in Shinjuku was like 22,000 円, but I was easily finding suitcases here in Ueno for under 5,000 円. I ended up going with a super cheap one for like 4,300 円, and made it back to the hotel, and called it a night.
