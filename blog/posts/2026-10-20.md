# Japan Trip - Day 10

Today was such a cool day. SO many cool touristy sites on the way to Aso. I left pretty early from Miyazaki, not too much to do there, so I just needed to leave. This time I wanted to actually hit some touristy spots, and Takachiho gorge was on the way to Aso, if quite a way down the road from Miyazaki.

## The road to Takachiho

On the way to Takachiho, I was just riding along and looked over to see a really cool moss covered bridge off the side of the main road. I pulled over to take a picture, and I could see a waterfall just behind the bridge... I thought, could I maybe walk to go see it? 

![A mossy bridge, over a waterfall, with lots of trees surrounding](/blog/images/bridgePreTakachiho.JPG)

So I took my pictures of the bridge, and then crossed it and went to go try and adventure to get closer to the waterfall. This took me a solid amount of time, especially because there was so much moss, and the rocks that I was walking on were all quite loose, so it took me time to gather my footing with every step.

But, it was worth it. 

![A picture of a small waterfall, with lots of trees and greenery around](/blog/images/waterfallPreTakachiho.jpg)

When I was walking across the stream, I then walked up the other side, to go to the other side of the bridge, and it was SO much easier. I can't believe I didn't take this route on the way down, it would've taken probably 5 minutes of my time off.

## Takachiho Gorge

I made it to Takachiho at a pretty reasonable time, and pulling into town, I went past quite a large shrine, with a large parking lto out front. I quickly decided "I'm going there next", and proceeded to the Takachiho Gorge entrance. On my first pass at it, there was a car stopped in the entrance, and the two guys manning it were basically shooing people away, so I figured "it must be full" and kept going in hopes of another parking option. After a few minutes, and no luck, I figured I'd take another pass at it, and this time I put my turn signal on, and they waved me through. Once I got down, I even saw a motorcycle specific parking lot, with the parking for bikes being immediately next to a beautiful pond. ELITE parking. 

![Heron in Takachiho pond](/blog/images/takachihoHeron.JPG)

The whole area was so pretty. The ponds a the top had Koi in them, and there was even a rock in one of the ponds with a huge Heron sitting on, undisturbed by the large crowds. Takachiho gorge itself was also really cool. The gorge was beautiful and much bigger than I expected, and there was a walking trail along the gorge that gave you many different views.

![Takachiho gorge, classic photo](/blog/images/takachihoGorge.JPG)

This was by far the most "touristy" spot that I had been to so far. The concentration of english speaking and chinese speaking people was HIGH. I did not speak to anyone. What's the fun in speaking english to some mega-tourist foreigner, in a mega-tourist area? I would love to talk to another American if they were in Amakusa, or Izumi, but if there's hundreds of them around in one area, we all know why we're here.

![My bike, parked right next to the pond](/blog/images/takachihoBike.JPG)

## Takachiho Shrine

After leaving Takachiho Gorge, I went straight to Takachiho Shrine, which I passed on the way in. There was a massive stone (or maybe metal? it was hard to tell) Torii gate outside, and the shrine itself was very large, and had some extremely old trees surrounding it. What a cool place!

![The Torii gate outside of Takachiho shrine](/blog/images/takachihoShrineTorii.JPG)

The path leading up to the shrine was really cool too, as there were lots of huge lanterns, and street-sign like things hanging on the way up to the shrine. No english here, so no idea what these were saying or implying.

![The path up to Takachiho shrine](/blog/images/takachihoShrineStairs.JPG)

![Takachiho Shrine main building](/blog/images/takachihoShrineBuilding.JPG)

## On, to Aso

I continued my journey to Aso after the shrine, and there were many great views along the way. I'll share one of my favorites, which I got when entering the Aso area, which is basically one huge crater, with the volcanoes in the middle.

![Aso entrance, from the south side](/blog/images/asoSouthViewpoint.JPG)

On arrival to the hotel in Aso, this hotel was one of the ones where you have to take your shoes off at the entrance to the hotel. No problem, I'm used to it at this point. I got into my room, which was a traditional Japanese tatami room, with futon on the floor pre-made. In the hotel description, it was explicitly stated that you'd have to make your own bed, but my guess is that the hotel saw my very not Japanese name, and made my bed for me. I would've liked to figure it out, but it also would've taken me a bit. The view from the hotel was fantastic.

![The view of Mt. Aso, from the hotel](/blog/images/asoHotelView.JPG)

I was starting to feel something coming on. A tiny scratch in the back of my throat. Uh oh. I decided to hit up a conbini, and make a health cocktail. I got a vitamin C booster, a multivitamin gummy pouch, and a cup of ice, and brought them to the hotel to make my cocktail.

![A special Lawson, in Aso](/blog/images/asoLawson.JPG)

When I got back to the hotel, I put on the TV, and it was on a kids TV show, showing a game show. At first, I thought it was just a Japanese game show channel, which I know are super fun, so I left it. The next game that was played though was an I-spy like game. I expected the kid on the TV to do the I-spy, but she just called out the number of hidden figures on the screen, and the locations weren't revealed. On the next I-spy, I picked up the remote and hit the arrow key, and the pointer on the live broadcast moved! Holy crap. I then proceeded to play I-spy, overlaid onto the live broadcast, just with the TV and nothing else. What a cool technology! I'd never seen anything like it before.

## Onsen #2

Aso is known for their Onsens, being a volcano town. I decided it would be good for me to get in the Onsen, and to do it at a later time to avoid the crowd. I went to the Onsen, and chilled out to end the day.
