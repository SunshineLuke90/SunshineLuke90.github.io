# Japan Trip - Day 8

Today, I started the day a bit late. I had a relatively short ride booked for the day, so I decided to sleep in, to give myself more time to rest, since I didn't need to make the trip quickly. The trip itself was uneventful, or at least uneventful as far as Japan goes, it's so pretty here that it's hard to constantly describe it. 

The ride ended in Kagoshima, with 20 minutes of riding along the coast, with the volcanic island of Sakurajima in constant view. This was a really relaxing portion of the ride, as it was such easy riding, not having to worry about leaves or sticks on the road, and having plenty of space between me and other riders. 

## Shrine & Park

I parked in Kagoshima at a local park, with a shrine directly nearby. I had been talking with my girlfriend for the past 20 minutes, so we continued to talk while I walked around the park. It was really peaceful, and there was a couple playing volleyball with their kids under the trees, which was really cute. I was very hot though, the jeans that I bought in Tokyo are very heavy and thick, and don't let air or sweat through whatsoever.

![A picture of a shrine, through a red Torii gate](/blog/images/kagoshimaShrine.JPG)

## Parking fiasco

After getting off the phone with my girlfriend, my goal for Kagoshima was to try some Shirokuma Ice, which Ayase San had recommended as a Kagoshima specialty. I had found a place online, which was in the downtown area, relatively close to where my hotel was for the night. I punched in the destination, and headed off on the bike. I got there pretty quickly, but I realized that it was on a strip, where it was walking only. So, to find parking! However, Kagoshima is another large-ish city, and if I've come to learn anything about large cities, it's that parking is impossible to find naturally. I found parking randomly, on the side of the street, and then punched in "Motorcycle parking" in hopes that I find something. Luckily, I did, a little bike parking spot on the side of the street. I locked the bike up, and started walking over to the Shirokuma ice place. 

## Shirokuma Ice

Firstly, I want to talk about Shirokuma Ice real quick. To explain why it's a fun name, you need to know a bit of Japanese. First, Shiro 白 (white), and kuma 熊 (bear). Together, white bear, or "polar bear". So, when you order a Shirokuma Ice, you're ordering a shaved ice dish, with the look of a polar bear in a cup. So cute!

I made it to the Shirokuma Ice place, and there was a huge line! Thankfully, the line was to get into the cafe, in case you wanted to sit down and have a full meal. There was a much shorter line which was separate if you just wanted a shaved ice, and didn't care about sitting down to eat it. I wanted to walk & explore anyways, so it worked well for me!

![A picture of the shirokuma ice that I ordered](/blog/images/shirokumaIceKagoshima.JPG)

The Shirokuma ice was so good too, and included some nice fresh fruit scattered on top, which went really well with the shaved ice/ice cream combo of the white bear. I began to walk around the strip, walking past several pachinko parlors (I plan to go to one, probably in Tokyo) and restaurants.

## Kagoshima Jazz Festival

Talking about me being lucky, Kagoshima just happened to be having their 10th anniversary Jazz Festival on this day. It was actually running through the weekend, so I was catching the end of it, but I was able to catch some full sets. I actually caught 2 full sets, before I decided I was just way too hot, and left to get my bike, and check into the hotel so that I could change.

![A picture of a Jazz festival stage, with a medium sized crowed](/blog/images/kagoshimaJazzFestival.JPG)

I grabbed the bike, and this time I navigated straight to a motorcycle parking spot, rather than to the hotel. I was able to find the spot right away, and park the bike super easily, grab all of my stuff, and check into the hotel on the 12th floor! I was lucky here too, as the hotel I checked into had a great view of Sakurajima. 

![The view from my hotel window, over Kagoshima, towards Sakurajima](/blog/images/kagoshimaHotelView.JPG)

I changed quickly, and went straight back out the door to catch more of the festival. I listened to the rest of another set, then went into the mall right nearby to look for stickers for my helmet. The mall was 4 stories tall, so I spent a decent amount of time looking through everything. It even had a library on the 3rd and 4th floor, which looked so cozy. I would've spent more time in there if I could read literally any Japanese.

![A cozy library, in Kagoshima](/blog/images/kagoshimaLibrary.JPG)

## Kagoshima Kurobuta

It was about 5:30 at this point, and I was getting hungry, so I went to the tourist information booth, which was right behind the Jazz festival stage, to ask for where I could get some good Kurobuta (literally - black pork). The worker pulled out a paper map, in all english, and marked our current location, and the location of the restaurant, noting that it was on the 2nd floor, up a flight of steps. 

I navigated to the restaurant, and got to the staircase to make it up into the restaurant, but I didn't really believe I had made it to an actual restaurant. I even walked in, and couldn't tell if I was in the right place. I was sat down, and was actually the first person in the restaurant. I may have accidentally shown up before they opened, but I couldn't tell. 

I ordered the kurobuta meal set, and it came out pretty quickly. The black pork was quite good, if a bit understated, and the meal set that it came with was very good, including a soup, some rice, and a little mini side dish, that seemed like coffee jello, with some creamer drizzled on top. Honestly, couldn't tell you what it was.

![A kurobuta meal set](/blog/images/kagoshimaKurobuta.JPG)

## Coffee with Kazu San

Now for the highlight of the day, while I was having dinner, I messaged Kazu San, the biker that I had met on the ferry the previous day. He had mentioned during our short conversation that he lived in Kagoshima, and so I figured I'd let him know I was in town, in case he had time and wanted to hang out. He messaged back pretty quickly, and recommended we get coffee & sweets at his favorite place in town. I was excited to talk more, and to experience a fancy local spot, so as soon as I finished my meal I went back to the hotel to grab my gear and ride over to the cafe. 

![A picture of the cafe counter and back wall](/blog/images/kagoshimaCafe.JPG)

![An Iced coffee, and a fruit ice cream parfait](/blog/images/kagoshimaCafeParfait.JPG)

Kazu San immediately introduced me to the cafe staff, it seemed like he was there often, and knew the staff relatively well, which was cool. It really felt like I was a local, being talked with not as a customer, but as a long lost friend that had been there for years. We talked about the differences and intricacies of the Japanese and English language, differences in culture between Japan and America, and they helped me with learning some new words and phrases. 

There was a lot of Google Translate back and forth, for sure, but being able to respond to things with Japanese rather than English every time, was so nice. Especially when I can communicate simple ideas and concepts with Japanese. Kazu San was so helpful, and having some others who were also interested in talking to me was really cool. 

We started talking around 7, and the shop was set to close at 9. When 9 rolled around though, Kazu paid our check (Super nice of him, he absolutely didn't have to), and I started to get up, but I was told to sit back down and wait. Turns out, the staff were having such a nice time talking with me that they wanted me and Kazu San to stay, and continue to chat. How sweet of them! I stayed, and we continued to chat as the staff were cleaning up the shop. Eventually we had to leave, but by that time, it was almost 11! Time really flew by as we were chatting, and it was such a nice conversation. I guess not too many foreigners try to have conversations with the locals, most of them said it was their first time really talking to a foreigner, which was a big shock to me.
