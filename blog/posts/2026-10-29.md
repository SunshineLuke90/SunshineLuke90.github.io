# Japan Trip - Day 19

I am running out of new areas to explore in Tokyo. Not really, but I've been trying to hit a new major area every day, and I've hit the majority of the massive ones at this point. I haven't been to the south center though, so I decided I would explore the Tsukiji market today, recommended to me before I started my trip.

Additionally, I had to do a room-change, due to a weird circumstance with my hotel booking. When I booked the hotel for 7 days, it booked one room for 6 days, and then a separate room for the final day. Kinda sucks, but my guess is that the hotel was fully booked on the 7th day, so it was the only way to make it happen. That, or the booking site I was booking through was just additionally cheap on me. Anyways, this meant that I needed to check my luggage in the hotel office, and clear my room before 10AM. This is why I needed to get the suitcase a couple of days ago, so that I could pack my stuff and easily move it on the 29th (today).

I brought all of my stuff down to the lobby, and checked my suitcase, duffel and helmet, no problem. I then headed out for Tsukiji market. As I walked into Ueno station though, I realized I was pretty hungry as I walked past a cafe, and decided to stop for breakfast. I ordered a honey butter toast and an iced coffee, both of which were really good, and whipped my laptop out to play some catchup on my blog, which I've neglected to write for the past couple days. 

As I began writing though, I was overhearing an older couple talking, and planning their day. The older guy kept hitting siri with back to back questions, like "Hey siri, how do I get to (Garbled mess of a name) station?" and this went on for a while, with various questions which were pretty simple questions. I am no expert in how Japan works, but I could tell that they were just starting their journey, and they could use some help, so I went over, apologized for eavesdropping, and asked if they would like any assistance along their trip. They were pretty happy to get some help, and were super sweet. They were from Texas, and it was the older guys 3rd time in Japan. They claimed that he had lived in Japan for a while, and that he actually spoke Japanese at one point in time. 

I was able to get them both a Suica card, so that their train travel would be easier throughout their trip, and I also took them to the station they needed to go to, and showed them how to navigate using purely public transit, using google maps and the public transit mode, talking about all of the patterns I've noticed in the Japan public transit system. Getting this interaction done was a really nice start to my day.

## Tsukiji Market

I made it to the fish market, and began walking around with no plan. I knew I wanted some sushi, but I also noticed there was some beer, fruits, and fancy chopsticks also being sold. After walking through as much Tsukiji market as I could find, I had a mental map and decided to hit all of the highlights that I'd seen. I went through, got a beer, got some sushi, got a fruit kebab, and got some chopsticks. What a haul. 

![The fruity beer I got in the market](/blog/images/tsukijiBeer.JPG)

The market itself is quite packed, but I was there a bit late (it's super busy in the mornings), so I can't imagine how busy it would be during peak times. The stalls all have 3 or 4 specialty things, so you can go all over the place and find different special items. It is really hard to figure out who has the best sushi, and what sushi is kinda wack, although I guess since it's in Tsukiji market it's all probably pretty good. The sushi that I got was extremely good, some bluefin tuna of different cuts. In terms of just the fish, it was definitely the highest quality raw fish that I've ever had.

## Roppongi Hills

I decided to head to Roppongi Hills. That's one of the big named destinations that I still haven't made it to, and it's somewhat on the way to Ueno, so it was an easy sell. I headed there, and then began to look for a cafe to get a drink and sit down to do some writing at. I found a starbucks, and ordered a "Sweet milk coffee" which I hadn't heard of before, but it was pretty simple and good, and I sat down to write for about an hour.

I walked around Roppongi Hills for a while, the area is nice, but nothing absolutely insane, just a lot of designer brands, so a bunch of stuff that I can't afford, and have no business even looking at. Because of that, I didn't spend a whole lot of extra time in Roppongi hills, and headed back to Ueno.

Back in Ueno, before heading back to the hotel, I was feeling a bit hungry, so I decided to head to McDonalds for a snack. I just got some shaka shaka fries, and a tea. I pulled out the laptop again to keep writing (I had 3 days worth of blogs to write, and these things are not the quickest thing ever to write), and just chilled out. It was nice that the McDonalds eating area was on a separate floor from the ordering area. I feel like that makes it easier to chill out without all of the beeping and whirring of the kitchen. 

At this point, it was around 4:20 pm, so I decided to go back to the hotel and move all of my stuff upstairs. I also needed to change, and work out how to pack my stuff into my bags, as I had gotten stuff at the Tsukiji market, as well as some candies on the way to the hotel from McDonalds. I also wanted to be able to get out at a reasonable time to make it back to Chikamichi, since I liked the experience so much the previous night. 

## Chikamichi, again

I decided early today that I wanted to go back to Chikamichi. They had some similar artists on tonight, and it's not like I had plans for anything else at night. I headed out, and made it to Chikamichi for the end of the first artists set. There was a bit of a larger crowd, and I recognized some people from last night, which was cool. Everyone is very isolated, and introverted, so no one tried to talk to me, but the band Kakuremono was so good, and their lead guitarist was clearly incredibly talented, so I had to tell him how good he was. I barely got my point across, but he seemed to understand, and was grateful, even shook my hand (Not common practice in Japan). I got a really good video of my favorite of their songs, but I can't share it here. I highly recommend checking out their music, they have an EP out on Spotify.

After the concert, I headed back to the hotel, and am now very tired. I may add more detail to this blog tomorrow, but I am calling it for the day now...
