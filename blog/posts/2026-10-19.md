# Japan Trip - Day 9

Today was a bit of a boring one. I woke up at a somewhat reasonable time, and rode out to Miyazaki. I started the day riding by taking the ferry to Mt. Sakurajima, a large volcanic island at the very south of Kyushu, somewhat bridging the gap between the two sides of the Kagoshima Bay. The ferry ride there was really cool, and the mountain was quite pretty, but I forgot to take photos (Sorry!). It was also quite hot, so in the back of my mind, I just wanted to make it to Miyazaki so that I could take off all of my gear and walk around.

I got off the ferry, and started to ride around Mt. Sakurajima, but my headset was disconnected from my phone after the ferry ride, and I missed the turn instruction early on to take a side road, which would've taken me on a more scenic route around the island. Because I missed it, I had to stay on a major road for quite some time, and before I knew it, I was already off of Mt. Sakurajima. I kind of figured "Oh, there'll be cooler things as I go anyways, this'll just give me more time" which made sense at the time, because there were no roads that took you closer to the top of the mountain, really just one or two roads that circle the base of the mountain.

But alas, there were no cooler things this day. I just kept riding, and there were some cool roads, but no majestic views or waterfalls or anything huge on the route to prompt me to pull over. It is what it is! More time in Miyazaki I thought.

## Miyazaki

I found my hotel pretty easily, and it had an attached parking lot, so I didn't even have to think about it (Nice!). The hotel was a bit weird, as it both wanted to be a virtual sign-in hotel, and also had physical keys that an attendant needed to provide. What's the point of me doing all of the check in myself on an Ipad if you're still going to have someone staffing the desk, and handing me a key?

I chilled out in the hotel room for a little while, just trying to cool down after a day of riding in such hot weather. Once I was ready to head out, I put on my pants (In Japan, you're looked at super weird if you're wearing shorts) and went to check out Don Quijote, as there was one only a couple blocks from the hotel. I arrived there, and went downstairs, and it was just basically a grocery store? This was not at all what I had expected. The only stuff on this floor was like meats, produce, chips, drinks, etc. I thought Don Quijote was a nick-nack store, or for gifts/souvenirs. I went upstairs, and there were definitely more daily item types of things, but nothing crazy. Maybe it's just because I had gone to the Bic Camera store in Tokyo on my first day, but the Don Quijote felt remarkably tame compared to what I was expecting. 

### Baseball Cap Side Note

By the way, the one thing I was actively looking for at this Don Quijote, and what I have been actively looking for since I started my trip, was a baseball cap, ideally one of a local baseball team. I don't need a nike hat, and I don't need a MLB baseball cap. Don Quijote had plenty of hats, but they were all MLB or NFL hats! Why when I go to Japan is the only baseball merch that I can find MLB merchandise? This is a rhetorical question of course, obviously the answer is Shohei Ohtani.

## Miyazaki Chicken Nanban

I had been told, in Amakusa, that Miyazaki was known for it's Chicken Nanban (or at least that's the specialty dish there). I looked online for places known for good Chicken Nanban, and found a smallish Izakaya that had Chicken Nanban as a special dish. I show up, and am able to get a seat at the bar, which is very lucky since the place is packed. There was a couple to my right, which were engaged in conversation, so I couldn't really try and start anything with them, and the guy to my right was on his phone the whole time. No good conversation to be had. The chef of the restaurant gestured to the counter in front of me as I was about to open the paper menu, and there was a QR code there. Website ordering, which is a huge plus for me, since I'd be able to translate it directly on my phone without having to use google translate.

The guy next to me had a dish that looked pretty good, so I found it on the menu, in addition to a beer, the Chicken Nanban, and a bread plate. The beer was HUGE, and came in a bottle, with a chilled metal glass to drink out of. Along with the beer, they provided a small dish with 4 pieces of jerky on it. This was a nice little touch, and the jerky was pretty good. The thing the guy next to me had came out first. I had thought that it was beef, thinly sliced, with a soy based sauce and greenery on top. I don't think that's what it was. In actuality, I believe it was either Octopus, Squid, or another tough seafood. I started eating it, but the meat had wildly different colors, within the same piece. Not in the way that you'd see skin vs flesh, but more like in the way you'd see cooked vs uncooked food. That, in addition to the meat being very tough, and generally being not what I expected, didn't start things well for me. 

The bread plate came out next, and was paired with a liver-based paste which came on a spoon. This was very good. Providing the spoon to make it easier to smear was a fantastic play, and not having the liver spread come out on the plate meant I got 100% of it, without having to scrape anything. The dish it came out on was a piece of slate, which was also really cool. 

The last thing to come out was the Chicken Nanban, shortly after the bread plate. After having experienced the mystery meat (only a mystery because I can't read Japanese, and it didn't translate well), I was worried that the Chicken Nanban would also be undercooked. I only had a pair of chopsticks, and the chicken pieces were far too large to eat whole. I took the first bite of Chicken, and inspected it... not very confidence inspiring. It wasn't like super pink, but it wasn't super cooked either. Not what I wanted to see after the previous dish. With that in the back of my mind, I couldn't focus very hard on the dish, and didn't really enjoy any of it. 

I think the food wasn't helped by there only being 2 staff running the entire restaurant (4 tables of 5+ people, plus a 6-8 person bar). One person was doing guest check-in and drinks, and the other was doing all of the cooking for the whole place. I'm sure it's not easy to cook for an entire restaurant all on your own, so I imagine he was rushing with the dishes. It is what it is. Not every place I eat is going to be a 10/10.
